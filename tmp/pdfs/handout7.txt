Satellite Navigation System
• U.S. Navy and Air Force programs combined to 
become GPS
– Basic architecture approved in 1973
– 1st satellite launch in 1978
– Fully operational in 1994 (22 years!)
• Other satellite navigation systems
– GLONASS (Russia), Galileo (EU), Compass (China)
• Called Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)

PAGE 5
Satellite Navigation System
• Global coverage(全球覆蓋) achieved by
– a satellite constellation of 20–30 Medium Earth Orbit 
(MEO) satellites spread between several orbital planes
– orbital inclinations of >50°
– orbital periods of roughly 12 hours 
– at an altitude of about 20,000 Km

Current status of GNSS
• Operational (In order of first launch year)
– GPS (US, 1978)
• Operational since 1978 and globally available since 1994
• GPS is currently the world’s most utilized satellite navigation system.
– GLONASS (Russia, 1982)
• Fully functional navigation constellation in 1995
• After the collapse of the Soviet Union (1991), it fell into disrepair
• It was recovered and restored in 2011
– BeiDou (China, 2000)
• A 16-satellite regional version (covering Asia and Pacific area) was completed by 
December 2012.
• Global service was completed by December 2018.
• On 23 June 2020, the BDS-3 constellation deployment is fully completed after the 
last satellite was successfully launched.
– Galileo (EU, 2005)
• Galileo became operational on 15 December 2016 (global Early Operational 
Capability (EOC))
• he full Galileo constellation consists of 24 active satellites, the last of which was 
launched in December 2021.
• The system is intended primarily for civilian use

How GPS works
1. The basis for GPS is resection from satellites
2. For resection a GPS receiver calculates
distance to satellites 
using travel time of radio signals
3. To measure travel time, GPS needs exact timing
4. Along with distance you need to know the satellite position 
5. For accurate positioning you must correct for errors

GPS Signal
• 3 binary digital codes are sent via L1 and L2 
carrier phase transmitters
– a. C/A code: from L1 carrier phase (1.023Mbps), for SPS
– b. P code: from L1 and L2 carrier phase (10.23Mbps), for 
PPS
– c. Y code: add Anti-spoofing(反愚) to P code
• Other message: 50 bps  ephemeris, satellite health 
and clock error

精確性(Accuracy)

Sensor Error (NSE), including two sources:
– Signal-in-Space (SIS) error is the part that 
cannot be eliminated from differential 
calibration
– Aircraft Instrument error due to receiver noise 
or antenna multiple path effect

完整性(Integrity)

accuracy is not sufficient, the capability 
for GPS system to send a warning signal 
– including Alert Limit, Integrity Level and 
Time-to-Alarm
– Alert limit contains lateral and vertical alert 
limits LAL and VAL

Determine RNP-
連續性(Continuity)
a. Lose of continuity means an interruption of 
satellite signals from satellite and ground 
stations 
b. Continuity is always trade-off with integrity 
at the same time

in probability at any time

distribution 
c. Airport conditions such as climate, terrain 
and equipment cause variation. 
Availability requires 0.999-0.99999
